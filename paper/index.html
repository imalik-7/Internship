Prioritizing Content Refresh Opportunities with Search Performance Signals

A leakage-aware ranking study that asks a practical question: when a content team cannot review everything, which already-visible pages deserve human attention first?

Abstract

Content teams have limited review capacity, so the practical question is not “which page is bad?” but which pages should be reviewed first? This study uses the FlyRank ML Internship warehouse to build a public-safe March 2026 feature frame from pre-decision search signals. A transparent CTR-versus-position baseline is compared with Logistic Regression, a small Decision Tree, and Random Forest using the same client-grouped holdout and Precision@K metrics. On that honest holdout, the best top-of-queue result reached Precision@50 = 0.30 versus 0.18 for the frozen Week-4 baseline, while the held-out positive rate was 0.179. The result is directional decision support for human reviewers, not proof that editing a recommended page will cause recovery.

Introduction / Problem statement

A reviewer can inspect only a small fraction of a large content portfolio at one time. A useful system therefore needs to rank candidates, provide a reason a human can inspect, and keep uncertainty visible.

Research question: Which already-visible pages should a content team review first for refresh or optimization, using only search-performance signals available before the decision moment?

The supported action is human review. A high score means “inspect earlier,” not “automatically rewrite.” The cost of a bad recommendation is wasted reviewer time, unnecessary changes to healthy pages, or missing a stronger opportunity.

Data

FlyRank ML Internship warehouse on Hugging Face, primarily fact_content_daily_performance. The model frame aggregates daily records to one pseudonymized client-content item.

March 2026, used as a mid-panel development month. Feature window: March 1–15. Outcome window: March 16–30. Population: items with at least 100 feature-window impressions.

Methodology

Features

Exactly five aggregates available before the outcome window:

  • impressions_feature15
  • clicks_feature15
  • ctr_feature15
  • avg_position_feature15
  • active_impression_days_feature15

Label / proxy

is_declining_proxy = 1 when March 16–30 impressions are more than 20% below March 1–15 impressions. It is a short-window decline proxy, not a label proving that a page needs a refresh.

Frozen baseline

The Week-4 rule prioritizes pages with at least 500 feature-window impressions, average position 1–20, and CTR at least 20% below the training-set median CTR for the corresponding position tier. Its score is visibility × CTR-gap severity.

Models and validation

Logistic Regression, a depth-limited Decision Tree, and Random Forest produce probabilities used as ranking scores. The primary evaluation is a fixed-seed 75/25 split grouped by client, so a client never appears on both sides. Baseline and learned methods are evaluated on the same held-out rows with Precision@10, @20, @50 and Average Precision.

Why the split matters: a convenient random row split reached Precision@50 = 0.80 for its best model, while the client-grouped evaluation kept for the paper reached 0.30. The large gap is evidence that random splitting can substantially overstate generalization when client-specific patterns repeat.

Leakage audit

All final features occur before the outcome window. The final feature list had an empty intersection with forbidden future/label/product-output fields. Existing system scores and flags are treated as baselines or context, never model inputs.

Limitations & honest framing

  • This is an observational, short-window ranking experiment.
  • The label is a later impression-decline proxy, not observed benefit from refreshing content.
  • The study uses one mid-panel month and five search-only features; it does not fully model seasonality, query mix, SERP changes, content quality, or business priorities.
  • Client grouping reduces memorization across clients, but a future multi-month time split would better mimic deployment.
  • The result does not prove Google's ranking algorithm and does not establish that a refresh causes recovery.

Safe interpretation: these scores support directional prioritization for human review. They should not trigger automatic deletion, rewriting, canonical changes, or publishing.
